Your steps

Who does what

PIFSC runs the data pipeline and sends you one file (pipeline_output.json) each year. You load it, review what looks unusual, answer two expert questions per flagged species, edit the paragraphs and create the document. Nothing you do here changes the underlying data.

What the flags mean

High this year's count is unusually high compared with past years — it needs a paragraph explaining why. Typical within the normal range. Low unusually low.

If something looks wrong

Every number traces back to the summary tables in step 4. If a value looks off, note the species and table and send it to PIFSC — the pipeline is re-run and you load the new file. Do not edit numbers in the paragraphs by hand.

For analysts

Check the statistics

Answers one question: if this were a normal year, how likely is a count at least this high? A small chance (under 5%) means the count is statistically unexpected. The sliders let an analyst test how sensitive that answer is.

Only higher-than-expected counts are treated as anomalies of concern.

Elicitation: calibrating α with the historical flag rate

For each α the table shows how many species would have been flagged in the current year and what share of the historical record (all species × years, each judged against the years before it) would have been flagged. A structured-decision-making elicitation with the Plan Team should set α so the implied false-alarm rate matches the review capacity of the Council process.

For analysts

Compare with the take limit

Scales the observed count up to the whole fleet and compares it with the incidental take statement (ITS). The result is a chance of exceeding the limit and a suggested posture. The adjustment sliders are for analysts and are capped so they cannot push the answer to a closure on their own.

Reference

Glossary & methods

Plain-language definitions of the terms used in this tool, followed by the technical method notes for analysts.

Glossary

Robust anomaly (MAD-z)

(current − median) ÷ (1.4826 × MAD) over the historical years. Immune to a single extreme year in the baseline. Gate fires at ≥ 2.

Rank percentile

Share of historical years strictly below the current value. Gate fires at ≥ 90 (i.e. the current year beats at least 90% of the record).

Trend model

Log-linear Poisson GLM with observed sets as an offset, 5-year window for seabirds and sharks, 10-year for turtles. A negative-binomial model replaces it when Pearson dispersion exceeds 1.5; a convergence failure is reported as "NB fitting error; Poisson fallback" rather than hidden.

Gamma-Poisson predictive

Prior shape/rate by method of moments (variance-matched when overdispersed). P(X ≥ current) = 1 − Ip(shape, current) with p = rate ÷ (rate + 1). Exact for non-integer shape; spreadsheet NEGBINOM.DIST silently truncates the shape argument. Validated against scipy.stats.nbinom and R pnbinom.

OR-gate decision

High if any gate fires. Gates are deliberately redundant: MAD-z is distribution-free, the percentile is scale-free, the Bayesian test accounts for overdispersion. Thresholds are parameters to be set by structured elicitation with the Plan Team.

Calibration model (Decision Agent)

Expanded take = observed ÷ coverage. Adjustments are multiplicative but each is capped (β ≤ 2.5, environment ±40%, gear ±30%) and combined through a single bounded factor, so they cannot multiply independently into a closure signal. Every factor is shown in the audit panel.

ITS running-sum tracker

Cumulative expanded take over the ITS window vs. (annual ITS × window years). P(exceed before window close) uses the posterior predictive for the remaining years. Alert at 0.25 per the working-group trigger specification. ITS values shown are placeholders.

Known pitfalls

  • Spreadsheet NEGBINOM.DIST truncates non-integer shape.
  • β-scaler × environmental index can compound; use one bounded factor.
  • Utility formulas break across species with very different natural abundances; scale by species median.
  • Rolling 5-year maximum ITS is legally practical but not population-referenced; PBR- or λ-anchored thresholds are the more defensible direction.
