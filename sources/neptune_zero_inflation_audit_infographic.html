01. The Two-Process Logic Architecture

Standard Generalized Linear Models (GLMs) smudge all zero outcomes into a single statistical average. In reality, zero-inflation in rare species interactions is driven by two distinct biological and operational mechanisms that require a two-stage hurdle approach.

Process A P(Take) = 0

Structural Zeros (The Absence Process)

Occurs when the marine environment is fundamentally unsuitable for OWT presence. If Sea Surface Temperature (SST) drops below $16^\circ\text{C}$ or primary forage is absent, habitat suitability is zero regardless of fishing effort.

Risk Management Impact: Assuming an area is safe forever leads to "False Safety" when oceanographic fronts shift.
Process B P(Take) > 0

Sampling Zeros (The Detection Process)

Occurs when sharks are present in the fishing zone, but by chance or gear depth, do not interact with the hooks. The habitat is highly suitable, but the stochastic event was not triggered during that set.

Risk Management Impact: Hides the "clumpiness" of risk, causing sudden cluster interactions when gear geometry changes.

Deconstructing the "Jackpot Fallacy"

The Jackpot Fallacy is the dangerous assumption that low observed average takes reflect a consistently low risk profile. In zero-inflated fisheries data, 95 sets can yield 0 takes, leading operators to believe access is safe.

However, when an oceanographic boundary condition shifts (such as the March 2026 Kelvin Wave), structural zero zones collapse into high-density forage traps. A single vessel can catch 15–20 sharks in a week, breaching annual compliance limits almost instantaneously.
